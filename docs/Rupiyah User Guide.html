1. Granting app permissions

Rupiyah needs a few permissions to work. Don't worry — they're all used for specific features you can turn on/off.

When you first open the app:
  1. Tap "Allow" if your phone asks about Notifications. This lets Rupiyah buzz you when a new transaction is found.
  2. That's it for now! The rest you'll turn on when you need them.

Notifications permission

Required for: alerting you when new transactions are detected

  • Android 13+: The app will ask you automatically. Tap Allow.
  • Older Android: Notifications are on by default. If they're off, go to Settings → Apps → Rupiyah → Notifications and turn them on.

SMS permission

Required for: reading bank SMS messages

  1. In Rupiyah, go to Settings → SMS.
  2. Toggle "Enable SMS monitoring" ON.
  3. Your phone will pop up a permission request — tap Allow.
  4. If you accidentally deny it, go to Phone Settings → Apps → Rupiyah → Permissions → SMS and set it to Allow.

Location permission

Required for: tagging transactions with where you made them

  1. In Rupiyah, go to Settings → Location.
  2. Toggle "Enable location tracking" ON.
  3. Your phone will ask for permission — choose "Allow all the time" (needed for background tracking).
  4. If you pick "Only while using the app", location will only work when Rupiyah is open.

Ignore battery optimization

Required for: keeping the email monitor running in the background

  1. In Rupiyah, go to Settings → Email.
  2. Toggle "Live email monitor" ON.
  3. The app will ask you to disable battery optimization — tap "Allow".
  4. If you skip this, your phone might kill the email service to save battery, and you'll miss transactions.

Picture-in-picture / overlay permission

Required for: quickly categorizing transactions without leaving other apps

  1. Your first time classifying a transaction, Rupiyah will ask for "Display over other apps" permission.
  2. Tap Allow to let the classification pop-up work.

2. Gmail IMAP setup (auto-read bank emails)

This is how Rupiyah reads your bank / wallet emails automatically. You'll need a Gmail App Password — think of it like a special spare key for the app only.

Step 1: Turn on 2-Step Verification (one time)

  1. Go to myaccount.google.com on any browser.
  2. Click Security on the left.
  3. Under "How you sign in to Google", find 2-Step Verification.
  4. Click Turn on and follow the steps. (You only do this once, and it makes your account much safer.)
Important: App Passwords only work AFTER you turn on 2-Step Verification. Do this step first.

Step 2: Generate an App Password

  1. Go to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords.
  2. Sign in again if Google asks.
  3. Under "Select app", choose Mail.
  4. Under "Select device", choose Other (Custom name).
  5. Type Rupiyah and click Generate.
  6. A yellow box will appear with a 16-character password (looks like abcd efgh ijkl mnop).
  7. Copy it! You won't see it again.
💡 Tip: The spaces don't matter. Rupiyah strips them automatically.

Step 3: Enter it in Rupiyah

  1. Open Rupiyah and go to Settings → Email.
  2. Tap Gmail IMAP.
  3. Enter your full Gmail address (e.g., yourname@gmail.com).
  4. Paste the 16-character App Password.
  5. Tap Save.
  6. Tap Test Connection to make sure it works. You should see a green checkmark.
  7. If you want the app to check emails in the background, toggle "Live email monitor" ON.

Step 4: Wait for bank emails

Rupiyah checks for new emails every 15 minutes. When it finds a transaction email from one of your trusted senders (you set those up later), it'll show up in the app.

Not working? See the Common problems section at the end.

3. SMS transaction setup

If your bank sends SMS alerts, Rupiyah can read those too. Works great alongside email.

Step 1: Find your bank's SMS sender ID

Open your SMS app and look for a message from your bank. The sender name is at the top — it might be something like:

  • HDFCBK for HDFC Bank
  • SBIBANK for SBI
  • ICICIBK for ICICI
  • Paytm or PhonePe for wallets
  • etc.

Step 2: Enable SMS in the app

  1. Open Rupiyah and go to Settings → SMS.
  2. Toggle "Enable SMS monitoring" ON.
  3. Grant the SMS permission when your phone asks.
  4. Under "Trusted SMS senders", tap Add.
  5. Type the sender ID you found (e.g., HDFCBK).
  6. Tap Save.

Done! Any SMS from those senders will be picked up automatically.

💡 Tip: You can add multiple senders. Add one for each bank or wallet you use.

4. Location tracking

Rupiyah can remember where you made each transaction. Handy for seeing "oh, I spent ₹500 at that shop near the office again".

Turn it on

  1. Open Rupiyah and go to Settings → Location.
  2. Toggle "Enable location tracking" ON.
  3. Choose "Allow all the time" for the best experience (the app logs location every 15 minutes in the background).

How it works

  • The app records your location every 15 minutes (only when you're moving).
  • When a transaction comes in around that time, the nearest location is attached to it.
  • You can see the location on the transaction detail screen.
  • Location history is stored only on your phone and is never sent anywhere.

To turn it off

Just go back to Settings → Location and toggle it OFF. All your saved locations stay in the app unless you clear the data.

5. LLM / AI setup (optional but recommended)

Rupiyah uses AI to understand messy bank emails. For example, if your bank sends "Trf to XYZ Rs 500" vs "UPI/DBT/1234 ABC", the AI figures out the amount, who it went to, and what category. Without AI, the app uses rule-based matching which works for most standard formats.

Option A: Groq (free, recommended)

  1. Go to console.groq.com and create an account.
  2. Click API Keys on the left, then Create API Key.
  3. Give it a name like "Rupiyah" and copy the key.
  4. Open Rupiyah → Settings → Intelligence → LLM Providers.
  5. Enter:
    • Base URL: https://api.groq.com/openai/v1
    • Model: llama-3.3-70b-versatile
    • API key: (paste the key you copied)
  6. Tap Save.

Option B: OpenAI (paid)

  1. Go to platform.openai.com and create an account.
  2. Add billing (you'll need a credit card).
  3. Go to API keys and click Create new secret key.
  4. Copy the key (starts with sk-...).
  5. In Rupiyah:
    • Base URL: https://api.openai.com/v1
    • Model: gpt-4o-mini (cheapest, works great)
    • API key: (paste the key)
  6. Tap Save.
💡 Costs are tiny. Processing hundreds of transactions costs a few cents per month. Groq's free tier is usually enough.

What is the AI used for?

  • Extracting amount, merchant, and category from bank emails/SMS
  • When the AI is unsure, it sends you a notification asking you to confirm
  • You can always edit transactions manually

6. Adding trusted senders

Rupiyah only reads emails from people/banks you trust. This keeps your inbox private.

  1. Open Rupiyah → Settings → Email → Trusted senders.
  2. Tap the + icon.
  3. Type an email address or pattern. Examples:
    • alerts@hdfcbank.com
    • noreply@paytm.com
    • @icicibank.com (caters all emails from ICICI)
    • @famapp.in (FamPay)
  4. Tap Save.
💡 Tip: Check your Gmail inbox for transaction emails and use those sender addresses.

7. Your first transaction

Once everything above is set up, here's what happens:

  1. Make a payment with your card, UPI, or cash.
  2. Your bank sends you an email or SMS (give it a minute).
  3. Rupiyah picks it up on the next check (within 15 minutes, or instantly with Live Monitor).
  4. If AI is set up, it auto-classifies the transaction. You'll get a notification.
  5. If AI isn't set up or is unsure, you'll get a notification asking you to categorize it.
  6. Open the app → the Home screen shows your balance and recent transactions.
  7. Tap any transaction to see details, edit the amount, change the category, or add notes.

Adding a manual transaction

For cash payments or anything the app didn't catch:

  1. From the Home screen, tap the + button.
  2. Enter the amount, description, and category.
  3. Choose Cash or a specific bank account.
  4. Tap Save.

Understanding categories & envelope funds

  • Categories are labels like "Food", "Transport", "Shopping" — you can add/edit/delete them in Settings.
  • Envelope funds let you set monthly budgets. For example, create a "Food" fund of ₹6000 and track how much you've spent against it.
  • When a transaction comes in, you can assign it to a fund. The fund balance updates automatically.

8. Common problems & fixes

Problem Fix
"Login failed" when testing Gmail connection You used your regular Gmail password, not a 16-character App Password. Go back to Step 2 and generate one.
"Web login required" error Google wants you to prove it's you. Open myaccount.google.com in Chrome, sign in, and do any security challenge. Then try again.
No transactions showing up • Wait 15 minutes — the app checks periodically.
• Make sure you added the sender as a trusted sender.
• Make sure the bank email went to your Primary inbox (not Spam or Promotions).
• Go to Settings → Email and tap Test Connection.
SMS not being picked up • Did you enable SMS in Settings?
• Did you grant the SMS permission? (Check Phone Settings → Apps → Rupiyah → Permissions)
• Did you add the SMS sender ID (like HDFCBK) under Trusted SMS senders?
Location not showing on transactions • Make sure location is enabled in Settings → Location.
• Make sure you granted "Allow all the time" permission.
• Location is logged every 15 minutes — the transaction needs to happen near that time.
App is draining battery • Turn off Live email monitor if you don't need instant checking.
• Turn off Location tracking if you don't need it.
• Set the email poll interval to 30+ minutes (advanced settings).
"IMAP error" or "Provider packing" error This is a rare app bug. Make sure you're on the latest version. If it persists, reach out to the developer.
AI isn't classifying transactions • Did you add an API key?
• Check the model name — is it typed exactly?
• Check your Groq/OpenAI account — do you have credits?
• Go to Settings → Intelligence and tap Test API.
How do I delete my data? Go to Settings → Data → Clear all data. Or uninstall the app (but backup first if you want to keep your data).
